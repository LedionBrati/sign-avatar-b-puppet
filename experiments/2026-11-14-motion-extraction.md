# 2026-11-14: motion extraction and finger-tracking quality (MediaPipe Holistic)

**Who:** Nikos (with Claude)
**Question:** Can free, CPU-only MediaPipe Holistic turn our reference clips into motion files (FORMAT.md v1), and how good is the finger tracking, honestly? (Issues #4 and #2.)
**Why it matters:** In Method B the motion data is the source of truth. If the fingers are wrong or missing here, no puppet or repaint can fix them.

> Nothing below says whether any sign is *linguistically* right. The numbers only say where the tracker is missing, jumpy or inconsistent. Only a Deaf or fluent signer can judge the signs.

## Setup
- **Inputs:** `references/how2sign/{judy,travis,dave,floyd}.mp4` (540x720, 24 fps, 142 / 147 / 202 / 277 frames; How2Sign CC BY-NC 4.0; signers 3, 3, 5, 5). Plus the first 20 signs of the WLASL library in `<signs>/` (25 fps, 414p to 720p, read in place, never copied).
- **Tools:** `mediapipe` 1.2.3 `HolisticLandmarker` (`holistic_landmarker.task` float16), VIDEO running mode, every frame, face blendshapes on. Python 3.13 via `uv`. CPU only: Intel i7-1355U (10 cores / 12 threads), no GPU. Free, no API calls.
- **Cleanup:** One Euro filter (hands `min_cutoff 1.0 Hz, beta 30, d_cutoff 2.0 Hz`; pose `1.0 / 10 / 2.0`; face `1.0 / 5 / 2.0`), restarted after every gap; gaps of up to 4 frames interpolated and listed in `cleanup.filled_frames`; longer gaps stay `null`.
- **Commands** (from `motion/`):
  ```bash
  uv sync && uv run python fetch_model.py
  bash run_how2sign.sh                       # extract -> clean -> report for the 4 clips into out/motion/
  uv run python batch_library.py --index <path-to>/signs/index.json \
      --out-dir ../out/motion/wlasl --limit 20 --workers 4
  uv run pytest -q                           # 24 tests, all pass
  ```
  Single clip: `extract.py VIDEO --out X.raw.json`, `clean.py X.raw.json --out X.clean.json [--trim-rest]`, `report.py X.raw.json X.clean.json --video VIDEO --out-dir DIR`.

## Result
- **Outputs** (all in `out/motion/`, git-ignored, regenerated by the commands above): `<clip>.raw.json` + `<clip>.clean.json` (about 2.2 MB each), `<clip>-report/` with `report.md`, `frames.csv`, `summary.json`, `<clip>-overlay.mp4` (source + pose/hands/face contour, lost hands circled red), `<clip>-skeleton.mp4` (source | clean skeleton side by side, the first "landmarks to skeleton" view for #4), `worst-*.png` hand crops.
- **Left/right convention, checked:** MediaPipe Holistic's `left_hand_landmarks` is the subject's left hand. On judy, the left hand's wrist is closer to pose wrist 15 (left) in 56 of 58 frames, the right hand's to pose wrist 16 in 129 of 129. The 2 exceptions are frames where the hands cross. So the output matches FORMAT.md as-is, with no swap. `--mirror` (flip before tracking) was checked on 2 WLASL signs against mirroring the data afterwards (`common.mirror_frame`): hand landmarks agree within 0.002 to 0.004 of the frame width, with the labels swapped as expected.

### Where fingers fail (frame ranges, what I saw in the crops and overlays)
- **Motion blur in fast moves and fingerspelling transitions** is the main cause. The hand is a pink smear and MediaPipe returns nothing. I could not read the handshape by eye either. Judy right f5-6, f8-9, f11-12 (the hand rising fast at the start), judy right f36-37, judy left f82-84 (a fast thumb-up), travis f13, f49-51, f62-64, floyd f136, f275, dave f32. 24 fps is too slow for these moves: the source is the limit, not just the tracker.
- **Hand at the mouth/chin:** travis left f62 and f98, floyd f174-179 (right, 6 frames) and f208. The pose model also puts its hand points too low in these frames, below the chin.
- **Hands crossing or touching:** dave f184-191 (right hand lost for 8 frames with the wrists crossed at the chest; too long to fill, so it stays `null` in clean), dave f153-154, f187, f194; travis f64-66, f98-100, f103. Travis's left hand also gets *mislabelled* while crossing: 7 frames where the "left" hand sits on the right wrist (f59-60, f71-73, f76, f104), plus 3 position jumps.
- Losses are short: 1 to 3 frames, except dave's 8 and floyd's 6. Gap fill (<= 4 frames) recovers most of them, but **filled hands are straight-line guesses**: in blurred frames (judy f84, dave f194, travis f62/f103) the interpolated hand is visibly off the real hand and the handshape is wrong. They are listed in `cleanup.filled_frames` so the puppet or a checker can flag them.
- Where the hand *is* tracked, the handshapes I looked at match the video: judy's thumb-up left hand (f100), the index+pinky+thumb shape at her face (f60, f100), floyd's held base fist (f148-174). Thumb and curl look plausible, but an extended thumb next to a curled index is where shapes visibly go soft in blur. Palm direction comes out right in the crops I checked. I did not measure finger spread.

- Cleaning cuts fingertip jitter by about 20-28% and keeps a median of about 89% of the fastest handshape changes.
- **The trade-off is real.** I swept hand settings on judy + travis. The first guess (`beta 10, d_cutoff 1`) removed 41% of the jitter but also 25-35% of the fast handshape changes. At 24 fps, real fingerspelling transitions and tracker noise share the same frequencies, so no temporal filter can separate them cleanly. I chose to keep the transitions and accept more jitter. The raw file is always kept, so this can be redone.
- Hand world points are re-anchored to the smoothed pose wrist after filtering (see "FORMAT.md" below), so the hand never drifts off the arm.

### Speed (CPU only, i7-1355U)
- Tracking alone: about **0.17 s per frame (~6 fps)** in an isolated benchmark (first 40 judy frames).
- Whole clips, one process: **0.8 to 4.1 fps** over 10 runs (typically 2 to 4). The laptop was shared with other running processes, hence the spread. Judy (5.9 s of video) took 35 to 103 s.
- Cleanup takes under a second per clip. A report with videos takes 1 to 3 minutes per clip, mostly drawing and encoding.

### Dominant hand
- All 4 How2Sign clips: **right** (judy and dave by motion, travis and floyd by height; in sentences both hands move a lot).
- WLASL first 20 signs: 7 right, 0 left, 13 unknown. Unknown is mostly two-handed signs where both hands move about equally (ABLE, ACCEPT, ACROSS, ACTION, ADAPT...). So 0 of these 20 were mirrored by the final detector.
- An earlier version (pose wrists only, left at ratio 1.25) called 5 of 20 "left" and mirrored them. A LOT and ACCEPT are two-handed signs where both hands move. ABDOMEN was plainly wrong: only the right hand is ever tracked, but the out-of-frame left wrist jittered. The final detector adds hand-landmark presence and motion, ignores wrists at the bottom edge, and asks for a clearer margin before calling "left" (ratio 1.75). That mirror run did execute end to end, so the mirror path works. But none of these 20 is a clearly left-handed signer, so the mirror decision is still unproven on real left-handed data.

### WLASL 20-sign batch (`out/motion/wlasl/summary.csv`)
- 20 of 20 processed, 0 errors. 1,175 frames in all (mean 59), trimmed to a mean of 41 frames by `--trim-rest` (for example letter A: 104 frames to 42-86).
- In the trimmed span, the dominant (or right) hand is detected in a median of 87% of frames (67% to 100%). On whole clips it is much lower (letter A 39%) because hands at rest sit below the frame.
- **Time:** 4 workers, 208 s wall for 20 signs = **10.4 s per sign** (5.7 frames/s aggregate, 2.2 frames/s per worker). **1,945 signs: about 5.6 h at that rate.** An earlier run of the same 20 under heavier machine load took 22.6 s per sign (12 h). Plan **6 to 12 hours, overnight.** The batch is resumable (`rows/<stem>.json`), and `--reclean` redoes only the cleanup in seconds. Output is about 1.6 MB per sign, so about 3 GB for the full library (in `out/`, not git).

### FORMAT.md: what the real output showed
- `left/rightHandWorld` are **not** hand-centred. MediaPipe Holistic 1.2.3 gives them in the pose world frame (origin between the hips). Hand point 0 equals poseWorld 15/16 exactly (distance 0.000 m in all 4 clips). FORMAT.md is corrected, and `clean.py` re-anchors smoothed hands to the smoothed pose wrist (`cleanup.hand_world_anchor`).
- `faceBlendshapes` has **51** names, not 52: MediaPipe has no `tongueOut`, and its extra `_neutral` is dropped.
- `cleanup.trim` = first and last kept frame of the raw file, both inclusive. `cleanup.filled_frames` = `{part: [frame indices in the clean file]}`. Clean frames restart at `i = 0, t = 0`, and `source.start_s/end_s` move to the kept span. Both are now spelled out in FORMAT.md.
- `motion_id` (added by the lead in PR #10) is written: `null` for the How2Sign clips, uppercase with underscores from the WLASL gloss (`a lot` becomes `A_LOT`).
- For WLASL, `source.file` is the path relative to the library folder (`videos/a-86f7e4.mp4`), since the video is not in the repo.

- **Scores, 1 to 5** (the template's rubric is for full videos; most items do not apply to a tracking step):
  - Correct signs: not judged (needs a signer)
  - Smooth between signs: n/a
  - Looks real: n/a
  - Speed: about 6 fps tracking alone, 0.8-4 fps per whole clip on this shared laptop
  - Cost (fal spend): $0
- **Stills:** `out/motion/<clip>-report/worst-frames.png` and the overlay videos (regenerate with the commands above).

## What I learned
- **MediaPipe finds the hands in about 90 to 98% of the frames where they matter.** The misses are short (1 to 3 frames) and have visible causes: motion blur at 24 fps, a hand at the mouth or chin, or hands crossing. Most can be filled, but a filled hand is a guess, not a measurement.
- **Fast fingerspelling is exactly where tracking is weakest,** the same weak spot Recast showed on day one (judy around 4 s). Smoothing cannot add information. Better source video (60 fps, good light, short shutter) is the real fix for the motion dictionary.
- **Labels can flip while hands cross** (travis, 7 frames), and the side-swap check catches it. Nothing fixes it yet.
- **Holistic's hand world points are in the pose world frame,** not hand-centred as FORMAT.md said. Their wrist equals the pose wrist exactly. Their size looks small (median wrist to middle fingertip 6 to 13 cm, even allowing for curled fingers), so the puppet should take finger *directions* or angles from them, not absolute lengths.
- **Telling dominant from non-dominant hand is ambiguous for two-handed signs.** "Unknown" is a fair answer there, and mirroring a symmetric sign does little harm. Calling a right-handed signer "left" and mirroring needlessly is the error to avoid.
