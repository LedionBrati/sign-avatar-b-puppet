# Findings, 14 Nov 2026: the first realistic signing avatar

Everything we tried on day one, what worked, what didn't, and the numbers.
Shared by both repos (A: clip stitching, B: 3D puppet + repaint).

---

## 1. The question we started with

Can a generative video model make a **realistic** person sign **correct** ASL?

## 2. The key insight

**Video models do not know sign language.** Ask one to "sign hello" and you get hands that look like signing but mean nothing. Deaf viewers would see nonsense.

So the signs must come from a **real signer**. The AI only changes **who** is signing, not **what** is signed.

```
real signer video  ->  AI swaps the person  ->  our avatar, same signs
```

## 3. The avatar

A made-up, photorealistic woman (not a real person). Made with `google/nano-banana-2.1` on fal, then 2 identity-preserving edits with `google/nano-banana-2.1/edit`. Prompts and rules: [`assets/avatar/README.md`](../assets/avatar/README.md).

Framing rules that made Recast work well:
- 3:4 portrait, waist up, facing the camera
- plain black long-sleeve crew-neck top (hands stand out against it)
- dark grey vertical-grain wall, soft window light
- no jewelry, no watch, no logo

We also tried an avatar made from a teammate's own photos. It worked technically, but we chose the AI person: no privacy risk, the team owns it, and nobody's real face signs a language they don't know.

## 4. The source clips

From **How2Sign** (real ASL signers, green screen, sentence-level clips with English text). License: CC BY-NC 4.0, research and non-commercial only. Details: [`references/README.md`](../references/README.md).

We picked 4 greeting sentences ("Hi, I'm ...") because they include **fingerspelled names**, the hardest test for fingers.

| Clip | Signer | Length | Body build vs avatar |
|---|---|---|---|
| judy | 3 | 5.9 s | similar (slim) |
| travis | 3 | 6.1 s | similar (slim) |
| dave | 5 | 8.4 s | heavier |
| floyd | 5 | 11.5 s | heavier |

Prepared with `pipeline/prepare-clip.sh`: cropped from 1280x720 to 540x720 (3:4), 24 fps, no audio. Crop x offset 380 to 410 so hands never leave the frame.

## 5. Two fal methods, 4 clips each

### Method 1: H3 Max Recast (`minimax/h3-max/recast`)

Takes the source video + 1 avatar photo. Swaps the person, keeps the motion.

| Clip | Hands | Face | Overall | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| travis | 5 | 5 | **5** | cleanest, T-R-A-V-I-S readable |
| judy | 4 | 5 | **4** | 1 to 2 ghosted frames in fast fingerspelling at ~4 s |
| floyd | 4 | 4 | 4 | keeps the heavier body build, avatar looks heavier and older |
| dave | 4 | 4 | 3 | same body issue, 1 s of resting at the start |

- **Signs: frame for frame identical** to the source. Output has the exact same frame count.
- **Time:** 1.5 to 3 minutes per clip (about 15 to 20 s of processing per second of video). 1080P was not slower than 768P.
- **Settings:** no prompt, `resolution` 768P for tests and 1080P for finals, safety checker on.
- **Limits:** source must be **5 to 30 s**, no single shot longer than 15 s.
- **Keeps from the source:** background (green screen), the chair, jeans, the signer's **body build**.
- **Weak spot:** very fast fingerspelling. The source hand is motion-blurred, and Recast can make it soft, see-through or briefly the wrong handshape for 1 to 2 frames. Facial expressions come out a bit softer than the real signer's (matters, because in ASL the face carries grammar).

### Method 2: H3 Max Reference to Video (`minimax/h3-max/reference-to-video`)

Makes a **new** video from the avatar images + the source clip as a motion reference.

| Clip | Result |
|---|---|
| judy | closest, 2 small sign swaps |
| dave | first 2/3 good, last 2.5 s drifts into other signs |
| travis | first half good, then generic gestures |
| floyd | weakest, the avatar photo's raised-palm pose leaks in mid-clip |

- **Looks just as real**, already on our wall (no background work).
- **Fast:** 8 to 23 s of GPU time, under 40 s total per clip.
- **But it interprets the motion instead of copying it.** The longer the clip, the more it drifts. Fine to look at, wrong for a Deaf viewer.
- **Pose leak:** the open-palm pose in `avatar-main.jpg` shows up as a made-up gesture. Fix that helped: pass references through `reference_conditions` in the order `[video, image, image]` (video first), same seed. It removed the leak and a 0.7 s lag, but did not fix late sign swaps.
- Results come with an AI-generated audio track; strip it.

**Verdict:** Recast for correctness. Reference to video only for "looks real" moments where exact signs don't matter.

Side by side: [`assets/demo/judy-real-vs-recast-vs-reference.mp4`](../assets/demo/judy-real-vs-recast-vs-reference.mp4) (left real, middle Recast, right Reference).

## 6. Finishing the demo

1. **Background removal:** tried `veed/video-background-removal` (h264, gives color.mp4 + alpha.mp4) and `bria/video/background-removal/v3` (ProRes with alpha). **Veed had cleaner hands** and kept less of the red chair. About 30 s each.
2. **Leftover red chair:** a few red pixels stayed. `pipeline/composite.sh` removes strongly red pixels in the lower part of the frame (lips are never touched).
3. **Wall:** an empty wall plate made with `google/nano-banana-2.1/edit` ("remove the woman, keep the wall"), so the background matches the avatar's original photo.
4. **Text:** our ffmpeg has no `drawtext` filter, and macOS `qlmanage` scales SVG text wrongly. **Headless Chrome** renders the caption, labels and credit as PNGs (`pipeline/render-layers.mjs`).
5. **Outputs:** [`judy-side-by-side.mp4`](../assets/demo/judy-side-by-side.mp4), [`judy-phone.mp4`](../assets/demo/judy-phone.mp4), [`judy-avatar-clean.mp4`](../assets/demo/judy-avatar-clean.mp4).

## 7. Things that did not work

- **Simple color key on the How2Sign green screen.** The green is pale and uneven (from `#5ca069` to `#a3d5a0`). Chroma key removed the black top; RGB color key left holes in faces and hands. Use an AI background remover instead.
- **One avatar photo with the hands up as the only reference** for Reference to video: the pose leaks into the video.
- **Heavier source signer + slim avatar** with Recast: the avatar inherits the body.

## 8. Money and time (rough)

- Avatar: 6 image generations. Wall plate: 1.
- Videos: 6 Recast runs + 8 Reference runs + 2 background removals.
- Whole day, idea to finished demo: about 6 hours, most of it deciding things.

## 9. Open questions this leaves

- How do we sign a sentence **nobody filmed**? (This is what repos A and B try to answer.)
- How do we make the moves **between** signs look natural?
- Can we get the same quality from a signer we record ourselves, with consent and a commercial-use license?
- How fast can it get? Recast is minutes, not seconds.
